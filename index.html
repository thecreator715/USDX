The process

From BONSAI to a dollar in four moves

1

Send BONSAI

You pay BONSAI to the receiver wallet. A memo marks the payment as a USDX mint request.

2

It is burned

The receiver returns that BONSAI to its issuer. On XRPL, tokens sent back to the issuer stop existing, so BONSAI supply falls.

3

USDX is minted

The USDX issuer pays you USDX worth the burned BONSAI at its market price. The receipt memo links to the burn.

4

The peg is held

The keeper trades the USDX pools whenever the price drifts from $1.00 and never lets it rest below $0.99.

The backing rule

Every USDX outstanding must be matched by burned BONSAI worth the same dollars.

USDX minted = BONSAI burned × BONSAI price in USD

Example: at $0.02 per BONSAI, burning 5,000 BONSAI mints 100 USDX. If BONSAI later falls in price, the coverage card shows the gap and one button burns the missing amount.

How the price stays at $1.00

$0.99$1.00$1.01

Below $1: the treasury buys USDX from the pool and sends it back to the issuer, which retires it.
Above $1: the treasury sells its own USDX into the pool. Trades are sized with constant-product math and carry on-chain memos.

Good to know

Is the burn reversible?

No. Burned BONSAI is gone from supply for good.

What does it cost?

No treasury fee, only the tiny XRPL network fee per transaction.

Who can mint?

Anyone with BONSAI and a USDX trust line. The operator’s processor signs the burn and mint.

Where are the keys?

Typed in at runtime, held in the tab’s memory only, never saved or sent anywhere.
